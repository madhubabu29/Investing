Sector PEG framework

These are rough practical ranges people often use when evaluating whether a stock looks fairly valued relative to growth.

How to calculate PEG ratio

PEG ratio is calculated as:

PEG = P/E ÷ Earnings Growth Rate

Example: if a stock has a P/E of 20 and expected earnings growth of 10%, then:

PEG = 20 ÷ 10 = 2.0

In practice, a PEG around 1.0 is often viewed as reasonably valued, while values well above 1.0 can suggest the stock is expensive relative to its growth.

Sector Fair PEG range Why
Software / SaaS 1.0–1.5 Recurring revenue, high margins, durable growth.
Healthcare / Biotech 1.0–1.8 Growth can be lumpy because of patent cliffs or trial risk, but margins can be very strong when it works.
Consumer Discretionary 0.8–1.2 Growth is often tied to the macroeconomic cycle.
Industrials 0.7–1.0 Capital-intensive, with growth often tied to reinvestment.
Financials 0.7–1.0 Earnings are influenced by rates and credit cycles; book value matters more than P/E alone.
Energy / Materials 0.5–0.9 Cyclical, commodity-price-driven growth.
Utilities Often N/A or >2 Growth is low and stable; PEG is not very meaningful here. Dividend yield and regulated ROE matter more.
