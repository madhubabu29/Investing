Model-based rating: HOLD / not a buy at this price

Rule used: compare the price with the three scenario values at a 10% discount rate. STNG's price of $85.94 is above the base-case value of $81 (conservative $65, bear $36). The price already assumes fleet-average earnings of about $30,000 per day or more, which is above both FY2025 ($25,964) and the FY2024–25 average ($29,269). The model finds no margin of safety, though the stock is not far above fair value if tanker rates stay elevated: at 8% the base case is $89, and at $34,000 per day the value is $104 at 10%.

What would change this: a pullback toward the conservative value (about $65–$71) or evidence that daily rates will stay above about $32,000. Main risk to the rating: the Middle East disruption that lifted H1 2026 rates to $44,902 per day could last longer than assumed.

What changed in this version

This page was rebuilt using only Scorpio Tankers' own filings (FY2025 20-F and the Q2 2026 earnings exhibit, both on SEC EDGAR). The earlier version used a 5-year average FCF of $550M and a 7.0% WACC taken from GuruFocus. The filings contain no cost of capital, so values are shown across an 8%–12% discount-rate range. The market price is shown only as a comparison.

Problems in the earlier method that this version fixes: (1) it applied past FCF to a fleet that has since shrunk from about 99 to 74 owned ships, while also counting the cash from those sales; (2) it ignored $978.2M of committed newbuild and joint-venture installments; (3) it treated the $605M 1.75% convertible notes (conversion price about $100.39) as neither debt nor shares; (4) it added back share-based compensation, which is about $44M a year inside G&A and $71.7M in total equity-settled cost; (5) it relied on a third-party WACC.

Method

Unlevered free cash flow per ship: (TCE per day × 97% revenue-day ratio − opex per day) × 365, less drydock cost ($2.3M per drydock on a 3.75-year cycle, about $0.61M per ship-year), less G&A. Each ship earns until it reaches age 25 (the 2014 builds in 2039), with no scrap value. The 12 MR and LR2 newbuilds are credited with earnings from their delivery quarter. Class rates are set by the FY2025 relationship between LR2, MR and Handymax pool earnings, scaled so the current fleet averages the scenario TCE. There is no growth, no inflation and no further fleet renewal beyond the order book. Equity value = present value of these flows − present value of the $978.2M installments + cash − debt, divided by shares. Convertible notes are treated as debt at face, and switched to if-converted treatment (+6.03M shares) only if value per share exceeds $100.39.

Caveats

  • The filings cover only recent years, so the bear rate of $20,000/day is my assumption rather than a filed figure.
  • The two VLCCs and the minority VLCC joint venture are paid for but earn nothing in this model, because the filings give no VLCC rate. Treating that 2028 spending as value-neutral would add about $4.4 per share.
  • Drydock is charged at a steady per-ship rate, which is higher than the company's own 2026–27 estimates.
  • Value is as of July 28, 2026, the latest filed balance sheet.
