Method

Unlevered free cash flow per ship: (TCE per day × 97% revenue-day ratio − opex per day) × 365, less drydock cost ($2.3M per drydock on a 3.75-year cycle, about $0.61M per ship-year), less G&A. Each ship earns until it reaches age 25 (the 2014 builds in 2039), with no scrap value. The 13 MR and LR2 newbuilds are credited with earnings from their delivery quarter; the four Hantong LR2s are spread across Q2–Q4 2029 because the release does not give each ship's quarter. Class rates are set by the FY2025 relationship between LR2, MR and Handymax pool earnings, scaled so the current 72-ship fleet averages the scenario TCE. There is no growth, no inflation and no further fleet renewal beyond the order book. Equity value = present value of these flows − present value of the $1,295.9M installments + cash − debt, divided by shares. Convertible notes are treated as debt at face, and switched to if-converted treatment (+6.03M shares) only if value per share exceeds $100.39.

Caveats

  • The filings cover only recent years, so the bear rate of $20,000/day is my assumption rather than a filed figure.
  • The four VLCCs and the minority VLCC joint venture are paid for but earn nothing in this model, because the filings give no VLCC rate. Treating the 2028 VLCC spending (about $540M for the four ships) as value-neutral would lift the base value from $73 to about $81.
  • G&A uses the latest quarter annualized ($147.9M). At the FY2025 level of $123.4M the base value would be about $4 per share higher.
  • Drydock is charged at a steady per-ship rate, which is higher than the company's own 2026–27 estimates.
  • The $38.4M purchase of Volare Shipping shares in October 2026 is not included; it moves cash into an investment at cost.
  • Value is as of Sep 30, 2026, adjusted for the pending ship sales and the declared dividend.
