Model-based rating: NOT A BUY at this price — moderate-high risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Salesforce, the price requires about 4.8% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $11.5B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $106 per share at 10% (price $248.53).

What would change this: A price below about $208 would bring the required growth under 4% (HOLD) and below about $144 under 2% (BUY). At the current price, free cash flow after share-based pay above about $13.4B a year (now $11.5B) would have the same effect as the 4% line.

Main risk to the rating: Debt rose from $14.4B to $39.3B in the first half of fiscal 2027 while buybacks were $27.3B (including a $25B accelerated repurchase in March 2026). Net debt is now about 2.4 times annual FCF after share-based pay, and share-based pay is $3.7B a year, about 23% of operating cash flow.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Share count and debt are both after the March 2026 buyback, so per-share values use the 823M post-buyback shares and the new debt. Using the old share count with the new debt would be wrong.
  • Capital spending shown is purchases of property and equipment; I did not find a separate capitalized-software line in the data.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2026 plus first-half FY2027 minus first-half FY2026.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.

These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice; I am not a financial advisor.
