Business model

Neurocrine is a San Diego-based neuroscience and endocrinology biopharmaceutical company that, until this year, was best described as a one-drug company with two smaller acts waiting in the wings. INGREZZA (valbenazine), a VMAT2 inhibitor approved in 2017 for tardive dyskinesia and later expanded to chorea associated with Huntington's disease, is still the engine: $716M in net product sales in Q2 2026 alone (up 15% year over year), with full-year 2026 guidance raised twice this year to $2.825–$2.875B. CRENESSITY (crinecerfont), a first-in-class CRF1 receptor antagonist approved in December 2024 for classic congenital adrenal hyperplasia, is now in its post-launch growth phase: $184M in Q2 2026 net sales, up 247% year over year, with roughly 80% of dispensed prescriptions reimbursed. The newest piece is VYKAT XR (extended-release diazoxide choline), the first FDA-approved treatment for hyperphagia in Prader-Willi syndrome, which Neurocrine acquired on May 18, 2026 through an all-cash, $2.9B ($53.00/share) acquisition of Soleno Therapeutics; VYKAT XR contributed $54M in its first partial quarter (pro-forma full-quarter run-rate of $94M). Total Q2 2026 revenue reached $959M, up 39% year over year, but the mix is still heavily INGREZZA-weighted (about 75% of the quarter), with CRENESSITY (~19%) and VYKAT XR (~6%, and rising as it annualizes) still small in absolute terms. Behind the commercial portfolio, the clinical pipeline is anchored by two Phase 3 programs — osavampator (an AMPA receptor positive allosteric modulator) for major depressive disorder, and direclidine for schizophrenia — both expected to read out in 2027.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a $2.9B cash-funded acquisition, a still-INGREZZA-dominated revenue mix, and a beta that looks too low to be true

Two things collide in this valuation. First, Neurocrine just spent nearly all of its accumulated cash pile — $2.5B at 2025 year-end fell to $482M by June 30, 2026 — buying Soleno Therapeutics outright in cash rather than with debt, in a single quarter reshaping both the balance sheet and the revenue mix (and adding a third drug with real launch execution risk of its own, layered on top of CRENESSITY's own 18-month-old launch). Second, and more consequential for the DCF math: NBIX's realized beta is startlingly low for a company that still gets three-quarters of its revenue from one drug and is funding two Phase 3 pipeline bets — 0.39 (stockanalysis.com / Google Finance) and 0.36 (Yahoo Finance, 5-year monthly), though other sources using different windows show more typical mid-single-digit-beta readings (TradingView 0.54, Motley Fool 0.64). Plugged mechanically into CAPM, a sub-0.4 beta produces a WACC barely above 6.6% — one of the lowest discount rates in this entire series — which, combined with NBIX's already-large and still-growing free cash flow, pushes the DCF to values dramatically above the current market price in every scenario except the bear case. That gap is the story this page has to grapple with.

The debt picture

Neurocrine carries no funded debt. It established a $1B senior secured revolving credit facility in connection with the Soleno acquisition, but it remains fully undrawn — a liquidity backstop, not leverage actually taken on. As of June 30, 2026 the company held $389.8M in cash and cash equivalents plus $91.9M in noncurrent marketable securities ($481.7M total) against $0 of drawn debt, for a net cash position of roughly $482M ($4.74/share) — down sharply from $2,543M at 2025 year-end because the Soleno deal was paid essentially all in cash. On the patent side, INGREZZA's regulatory exclusivity runs through 2030 and its core valbenazine composition-of-matter patent (with patent-term extension) runs to 2031, but 2023 settlement agreements with ANDA filers push actual generic market entry out to March 1, 2038 (or earlier only under specified conditions) — a longer runway than most single-drug biotechs get, but one that sits just past the 10-year explicit forecast window used here, meaning the terminal-value assumption implicitly treats INGREZZA's economics as still intact through year 10 (2036) without yet pricing in the cliff two years later.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Each scenario anchors on a different FCF base to bracket how much of the recent acceleration — CRENESSITY's post-launch ramp, a fresh (and only partially annualized) VYKAT XR contribution, and INGREZZA's own guidance raises — is durable. The base case uses the TTM run-rate through June 30, 2026 and assumes double-digit growth continues for several years as CRENESSITY and VYKAT XR scale and INGREZZA keeps compounding at its guided pace, before decelerating. The conservative case starts from FY2025's actual FCF — before VYKAT XR was even part of the company — and assumes growth moderates to high-single digits as CRENESSITY's triple-digit growth normalizes and Soleno integration costs (R&D and SG&A guidance were both raised this year) weigh on margins. The bear case starts from the smoothed 5-year average FCF, which still carries the weight of NBIX's lower-margin earlier years, and assumes low-single-digit growth reflecting continued INGREZZA concentration, integration execution risk, and the eventual patent cliff. Every scenario adds net cash ($482M) and divides by 101.6M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

Unlike most names in this series, where the market price lands between the base and bear cases, NBIX's $154.06 sits below every scenario except the bear case — including the conservative case, which already assumes CRENESSITY's growth normalizes sharply and Soleno integration costs bite. That gap traces almost entirely to WACC: a mechanically low, CAPM-derived discount rate built on a beta of 0.36–0.39 treats NBIX as one of the least risky names in this entire series, which is hard to square with a company that still gets three-quarters of its revenue from one drug, just spent nearly its whole cash balance on an acquisition with its own launch-execution risk, and has two binary Phase 3 readouts (osavampator, direclidine) due in 2027. If the market is implicitly using a WACC several points higher than CAPM's — pricing in single-drug concentration and pipeline binary risk that a trailing-price-based beta simply doesn't capture — then something between the conservative and bear case is a more honest read of what NBIX is actually worth today, and the base case's +165% gap says more about the fragility of low-beta CAPM outputs than about Neurocrine being dramatically undervalued.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC, and this page is a clear illustration of that: a beta below 0.4 nearly halves the discount rate relative to a typical single-product biotech in this series (compare AUPH's 11.7%), which by itself explains most of the gap between the base case and the market price. The Soleno acquisition closed mid-quarter, so TTM figures blend legacy INGREZZA/CRENESSITY-only economics with only a partial quarter of VYKAT XR — not a clean run-rate — and the 10-year explicit window ends two years before the 2038 generic-entry date implied by INGREZZA's ANDA settlements. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
