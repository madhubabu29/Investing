Model-based rating: NOT A BUY at this price — high risk

Rule used for cyclical companies: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value. At 10%, Valero's base value (3-year average) is $157 and its run-rate value (latest twelve months) is $295. The $419 price is above both: it needs about $12.7B of free cash flow a year after share-based pay, forever, versus $9.2B over the latest twelve months, which was itself the strongest period in these filings.

What would change this: a price below about $157 would be a BUY on the 3-year average, and a price below about $295 a HOLD. Main risk to the rating: refining profits swing widely: operating income was $11.9B in 2023, $3.2B in 2025, and $6.9B in the first half of 2026 alone (versus $0.1B in the first half of 2025). Working-capital swings also move operating cash flow, so the run-rate may overstate what lasts.

Method

Refining is cyclical, so the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF at four levels taken from the filings (g = 0), plus cash less debt less non-controlling interest, divided by shares. The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years; Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months (fiscal 2025 plus first half 2026 minus first half 2025). Diamond Green Diesel is consolidated at 100%, so its partner's share is deducted at book value.
