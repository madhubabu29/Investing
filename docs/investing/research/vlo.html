Business model

Valero is one of the world's largest independent petroleum refiners, converting crude oil into gasoline, diesel, and jet fuel across its refining network, plus a renewable diesel and ethanol business. Revenue is largely a pass-through of crude oil costs; profitability comes from the spread between crude input costs and refined product prices (the "crack spread"), not from volume growth. Thin FCF margins (3–6% of revenue) are normal for this business — the dollar amounts of profit can still be large because revenue itself is enormous.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a second margin spike inside five years

FY2022 was the prior refining-margin peak: Russia's invasion of Ukraine disrupted global refined-product trade routes, and FCF nearly tripled to $9.84B. Margins normalized down every year after — FCF fell to $4.63B (2024) and then $3.94B (2025). Now, in 2026, a fresh supply shock has hit: Ukrainian drone strikes on Russian refineries plus U.S. refinery closures have again constrained global refining capacity, pushing the U.S. 3-2-1 crack spread to roughly $59/barrel — nearly tripling since January 2026. Q2 2026 alone generated $5.58B of operating cash flow (more than the entirety of 2021), and trailing-twelve-month FCF (through June 2026) is approximately $9.29B — more than double FY2025's figure and back near the 2022 peak.

The debt picture

Net debt is modest at $1.23B ($4.26/share) and easily serviced out of a single quarter's cash flow in the current environment. Valero has also been returning significant capital to shareholders — roughly $2.6B of buybacks in FY2025 alone — visible in the shrinking share count (305.0M a year ago down to 287.9M now). WACC estimates range from 7.44% to 8.91% across sources.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Given how much a single year (or quarter) can mislead for a refining cyclical, each scenario anchors on a different base: the current TTM run-rate, the smoothed 5-year average (which spans both the 2022 and 2026 spikes), and the FY2025 actual (the most recent full year before this year's margin surge). Every scenario subtracts net debt ($1.23B) and divides by 287.9M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

Unlike the AI-supercycle names covered elsewhere on this site, VLO lands in a familiar, more typical pattern: the market price sits between the conservative and base cases. That implies the market is pricing a partial — not full — continuation of the current margin spike, which is consistent with how refining cycles have historically played out: elevated crack spreads eventually draw new capacity online or disrupted supply comes back, and margins fade. The 2022 cycle is the direct precedent — FCF nearly tripled, then fell for three straight years afterward. The DCF can't tell you whether 2026's capacity destruction (war damage, permanent plant closures) proves more durable than 2022's was; that's a judgment call about the global refining supply balance, not something a growth-rate assumption resolves.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC, and for a refining cyclical it is at least as sensitive to which historical period is chosen as the starting base. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
