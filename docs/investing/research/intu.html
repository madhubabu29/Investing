Model-based rating: HOLD — moderate risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Intuit, the price requires about 2.9% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $6.6B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $234 per share at 10% (price $343.36).

What would change this: A price below about $302 would bring the required growth to 2% or lower (BUY); a price above about $414 would push it over 4% (NOT A BUY).

Main risk to the rating: Fiscal 2026 operating cash flow jumped 42% to $8.8B, so the run-rate is a single strong year; the fiscal 2024 figure was $2.7B after share-based pay. Share-based pay is $2.1B a year, about 23% of operating cash flow.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Funds held for customers and the related investments are excluded; they belong to customers and are offset by liabilities.
  • Capex includes purchases of property and equipment plus capitalized internal-use software ($46M in FY2026).
  • If Intuit holds short-term investments I could not retrieve, net debt would be lower and value per share higher.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
