Model-based rating: BUY (value signal) — moderate risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Intuit, the price requires about 0.4% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $7.0B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $259 per share at 10% (price $270.44).

What would change this: A price above about $332 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), above about $453 above 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $550 above 5% (SELL). At the current price, FCF after share-based pay falling below about $5.7B a year (now $7.0B) would move it to HOLD, and below about $4.2B to NOT A BUY.

Main risk to the rating: The BUY rests on the latest twelve months holding. FCF after share-based pay was $2.7B in fiscal 2024, $4.1B in 2025 and $6.6B in 2026, so the 3-year average is only $4.5B; valued on that average, with no growth, the stock is worth about $163 a share. Operating cash flow jumped 42% in fiscal 2026 and the Q1 FY2027 quarter ($1,062M vs $637M a year earlier) is only one quarter. Intuit also cut its fiscal 2027 revenue guidance on Nov 19 (now $23,081M to $23,365M, 8% to 9% growth), citing a change to Credit Karma. Share-based pay is about $2.0B a year, 22% of operating cash flow.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Cash here includes $1,279M of current investments, which Intuit itself counts in its "cash and investments" total of $5.3B. The earlier version of this page counted cash only (no investment line was retrieved). On cash only, net debt would be $2.9B, implied growth 0.5% and the no-growth value $254 a share; the rating does not change.
  • Funds receivable and amounts held for customers ($5,749M) are excluded; they belong to customers and are offset by liabilities.
  • Mailchimp: Intuit agreed on Nov 19 to sell it to Bending Spoons for about $3.68B ($3.31B cash and $368M in Bending Spoons stock), expected to close in early calendar 2027, with the proceeds to go to share repurchases. The numbers above are before the sale. Mailchimp's own cash flow is inside the twelve-month figure, and the filings used do not separate it.
  • Capital spending is purchases of property and equipment. The $46M of capitalized internal-use software is the fiscal 2026 figure; the 10-Q has no separate line for it, so it is held at the annual amount in the twelve-month figure.
  • Intuit lends to customers; loan originations, sales and repayments sit in investing activities and are not deducted from FCF here (Q1: $1.8B originated, $1.8B repaid or sold). Operating cash flow adds back $64M of credit-loss provisions in Q1.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2026 plus Q1 FY2027 minus Q1 FY2026.
  • Starting in Q1 FY2027 Intuit no longer excludes share-based pay from its non-GAAP measures. This page has always deducted it.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
