Model-based rating: BUY (value signal) — high risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For lululemon, the price requires about -3.4% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $1,263M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $133 per share at 10% (price $99.04).

What would change this: A price above about $166 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), and above about $220 above 4% (NOT A BUY). A further drop in free cash flow would weaken the case: at the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay falling below about $0.71B (now $1.26B) would remove the BUY.

Main risk to the rating: The earnings trend is down: operating income fell from $2.51B to $2.21B in fiscal 2025, and the first half of fiscal 2026 was $731M versus $962M a year earlier. Free cash flow after share-based pay fell 42% in fiscal 2025 ($1.49B to $0.86B) before recovering to $1.26B over the latest twelve months, helped by a $380M improvement in half-year operating cash flow (working capital timing is not explained by the data used). Capital spending is a large $0.63B a year.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.
