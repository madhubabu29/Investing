Model-based rating: BUY (value signal) — high risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For lululemon, the price requires about -2.4% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $1,263M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $127 per share at 10% (price $102.62).

What would change this: A price above about $158 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), above about $210 above 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $252 above 5% (SELL). A further drop in free cash flow would weaken the case: at the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay falling below about $0.78B (now $1.26B) would move it to HOLD, and below about $0.58B to NOT A BUY.

Main risk to the rating: The earnings trend is down: operating income fell from $2.51B to $2.21B in fiscal 2025, and the first half of fiscal 2026 was $731M versus $962M a year earlier. In Q2 revenue fell 4% to $2.4 billion and comparable sales fell 9%. Free cash flow after share-based pay fell 42% in fiscal 2025 ($1.49B to $0.86B) before recovering to $1.26B over the latest twelve months, helped by a $380M improvement in half-year operating cash flow (working capital timing is not explained by the data used). Valued on the 3-year average ($1.30B) the stock is worth about $130 with no growth, so the BUY depends on the recovery holding. Capital spending is a large $0.63B a year, and operating lease liabilities of $2.1B are not counted as debt.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Correction: the earlier version of this page used 105.6M shares and left out the 5.1M exchangeable shares of Lulu Canadian Holding, which carry the same economic rights as common shares. Including them lowers every per-share value by about 4.6%.
  • The latest filing is still the Q2 FY2026 10-Q (quarter ended Aug 2, 2026). Q3 results are scheduled for Dec 10, 2026, so they are not in these numbers.
  • Share-based pay for the first half of fiscal 2026 ($50.3M) was more than double the prior-year half ($21.2M).
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2025 plus first half of fiscal 2026 minus first half of fiscal 2025.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
