Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • The company announced in its Nov 5, 2026 earnings release that it expects to enter a $500M accelerated share repurchase in the week of Nov 9, 2026. That is not in the filings used here: the net cash and share count above are before it. Buying shares at a fair price roughly leaves value per share unchanged, but it lowers net cash by about $500M and the share count by about 3 million.
  • No capitalized software line appears in the cash flow statements, so capital spending is purchases of property and equipment only.
  • Customer prepayments and acquisition-related contingent consideration are not subtracted.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: 2025 plus nine months 2026 minus nine months 2025.
  • The price is the Nov 18, 2026 close of $154.16 (Benzinga), the latest I could verify; the Nov 20 close was not found.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
