Business model

EPAM is a digital engineering and IT consultancy that designs and builds custom software for enterprise clients in financial services, travel, life sciences, and software/hi-tech, among others. Unlike broader consultancies (ACN, CTSH), EPAM's model is more purely hands-on-keyboard custom software delivery, historically staffed from a large, cost-advantaged engineering base in Belarus, Ukraine, and Russia — a base the company has spent the last four years diversifying toward India, Latin America, and elsewhere following the 2022 Russia-Ukraine war disruption to its founding markets. Revenue is largely time-and-materials and fixed-fee engagements, and the business is asset-light, with capex never exceeding 2.4% of revenue in any of the last five years.

The defining challenge for this DCF — AI-disruption fear meets an aggressive buyback, plus a cash-flow air pocket

EPAM stock is down roughly 85% from its all-time high of $717 (November 2021), including a fresh 49% leg down since November 2025, after management cut full-year 2026 revenue growth guidance to 3.2%–4.2% amid softening demand and client hesitancy tied to AI uncertainty. As a hands-on-keyboard custom software shop, EPAM sits closer to the center of the AI-code-generation disruption thesis than broader consultancies — the same coding assistants and agentic tools that could compress the billable-hours model for competitors hit EPAM's core delivery work most directly. Management's answer mirrors the playbook seen elsewhere in this series (Adobe leaning into Firefly, Accenture into GenAI bookings): lean into AI-native delivery rather than resist it. EPAM generated over $105M of AI-native revenue in Q4 2025 alone and guides to more than $600M for full-year 2026 — meaning the same disruption narrative shows up as both a risk and a growth line in the same set of numbers. Compounding the read, operating cash flow swung negative in H1 2026 (−$38.8M, versus +$77.4M in H1 2025) as trade receivables jumped $172.2M and accrued compensation fell $153.6M — a working-capital timing issue per the 10-Q, not an obviously structural change, but one that makes the "current run-rate" an unusually unreliable anchor this cycle. Management's own read is that the selloff is overdone: the company has repurchased $409M of stock in H1 2026 alone (including a $300M accelerated repurchase started in March 2026) under a $1B authorization, buying back stock at a P/E near 11–14x against a 3-year average of about 28x and a 5-year average near 39x.

The cash picture

Net cash stands at $637.2M ($12.35/share) as of June 30, 2026, down from $1,152M ($22.33/share) at FY2025-end — the decline is entirely the buyback plus the working-capital cash drag, not new debt (total debt is roughly flat near $150M, almost all lease liabilities). GuruFocus puts EPAM's WACC at 11.56%, essentially at its own 10-year median, and separately estimates a GF Value of $258.28 — "significantly undervalued" by their own multiple-based framework, well above even this page's bullish DCF case.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Each scenario anchors on a different FCF base, ordered from most to least optimistic: FY2025 actual (a clean, record year, pre-guidance-cut), the smoothed 5-year average (spanning the 2022 workforce-disruption trough and the 2025 rebound), and the TTM run-rate (which fully absorbs this year's negative-OCF working-capital swing). The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

The market price sits just below the conservative case (only 8% of upside there) and well above the bear case — meaning the market is pricing something close to "today's depressed, working-capital-dented run-rate is roughly the new normal, with modest growth resuming," not a full structural collapse of the custom-engineering model. Getting to the base case requires believing FY2025's record results were the real trend and this year's guidance cut and cash-flow air pocket are both temporary — a bigger ask here than for the broader consultancies covered elsewhere in this series, precisely because EPAM's core delivery work is more directly exposed to AI code-generation tools. The $600M-plus of guided AI-native revenue is real evidence management is finding a place in the new model, and a $409M buyback at today's prices is a concrete bet by the people with the best information that the market has overshot. Whether that AI-native pivot is a durable second act or the same billable-hours business relabeled, and whether the H1 2026 cash flow dip reflects one-time collections timing or the start of real demand softness, is exactly the kind of judgment call a mechanical DCF can't settle on its own.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC, and unusually so here given how much the choice of FCF base (FY2025 actual vs. TTM) swings the result — a direct consequence of this year's working-capital volatility. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
