Model-based rating: NOT A BUY — high risk

Rule used for cyclical companies: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value; SELL if it is more than twice the run-rate value (about $267). At 10%, EOG's base value (3-year average FCF) is $84 and its run-rate value (twelve months to Sep 30, 2026) is $134. The $148.60 price is above both. It needs about $7.9B of free cash flow every year, forever, against $7.1B over the last twelve months and $3.2B in 2025. The model is therefore paying for current commodity prices to last, with no allowance for them falling.

What would change this: a price below about $134 would be a HOLD, and below about $84 a BUY. At today's price, sustained FCF of about $7.9B a year would put the price at fair value on this method. Main risk to the rating: FCF swung from $3.2B in 2025 to $7.1B over the last twelve months, mostly because oil and gas prices were higher. NYMEX crude averaged $83.62 a barrel in the first nine months of 2026, up 25%, according to the 10-Q. A fall back toward 2025 levels would cut the value sharply (see the sensitivity table).

What changed in this version

This page was rebuilt using only EOG's SEC filings (the 2025 10-K and the Q3 2026 10-Q). The earlier version used a CAPM-derived cost of capital (6.1%, from a beta of 0.28) and a price from Aug 28, 2026, and it did not deduct share-based pay. The filings contain no cost of capital, so values are shown across an 8%–12% range, with the market price used only as a comparison. Share-based compensation is treated as a real cost: FCF here is operating cash flow − capital spending − share-based compensation.

Net debt is now $2.2B (cash $5.7B, debt $7.9B) at Sep 30, 2026, against $3.0B at Jun 30 in the earlier version. Debt is almost all senior notes ($7.89B principal); the 10-Q does not show revolver borrowings, and the earlier version reported none drawn at Jun 30. Debt-to-total-capitalization is 19%. If you prefer companies with little or no debt, EOG is low on debt for an oil and gas producer but not debt-free.

Method

Because oil and gas prices make EOG's cash flow swing from year to year, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, minus net debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three years; Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, calculated as 2025 plus nine months of 2026 minus nine months of 2025. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate.

Caveats

  • The run-rate period includes about nine months of high commodity prices; the 2025 trough was a lower-price year. Neither is a forecast.
  • The 2025 figure includes only part of a year of Encino production (the deal closed in August 2025), while the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026 include it in full. The $4.45B purchase price is not deducted from FCF.
  • Asset sale proceeds ($394M in nine months of 2026, plus $180M after quarter-end) are excluded from FCF and from cash.
  • Operating lease liabilities are not counted as debt. The current portion alone is $340M, about $0.66 a share; I did not extract the non-current portion.
  • Any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a producer that grows volumes (EOG guides to about 14% total volume growth in 2026) will be worth more than shown at the same prices.
  • The rating holds for any price between $134 and $267.
