Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Monster Beverage, the price requires about 5.0% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $1,962M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $24 per share at 10% (price $46.20).

What would change this: A price below about $39 would bring the required growth under 4% (HOLD) and below about $30 under 2% (BUY). At the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay above about $2.37B (now $1.96B) would have the same effect as the 4% line.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • No debt line was returned, so debt is treated as zero; if there is any, value per share would be slightly lower.
  • Long-term investments ($781M) are counted as cash-like; they are held in Treasury, agency and corporate bonds.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: 2025 plus first half 2026 minus first half 2025.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
