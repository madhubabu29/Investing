Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Cirrus Logic, the price requires about 0.0% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $446M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $111 per share at 10% (price $110.61).

What would change this: A price above about $136 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), above about $177 above 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $210 above 5% (SELL). At the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay falling below about $348M (now $446M) would move it to HOLD, below about $256M to NOT A BUY, and below about $211M to SELL.

Main risk to the rating: Apple accounted for about 93% of net sales in the September 2026 quarter (90% a year earlier), so the whole cash flow rests on one customer. Cash flow is also slowing: operating cash flow for the first six months of fiscal 2027 was $115.0M against $208.3M a year earlier, and the 10-Q cites a $148.3M unfavorable change in working capital (higher receivables, inventories and other assets). Twelve-month FCF ($446M) is already below fiscal 2026 ($554M). The IRS is also disputing fiscal 2017-2019 transfer pricing: it asserts about $168.3M of additional tax plus about $63.7M of penalties (about $4.65 a share together, before interest); the company has not accrued it and is in Appeals. That amount is not deducted from the values here.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Marketable securities, including $324M held as long-term, are counted as cash-like. About $21.6 a share of each per-share value is cash.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2026 plus six months of fiscal 2027 minus six months of fiscal 2026.
  • Capital spending is purchases of property, equipment and software plus investments in technology.
  • The first half of fiscal 2027 had weak cash conversion: $52M of FCF after SBC against $160M a year earlier, while net income was flat. The 10-Q attributes this to working capital. If it reverses, the twelve-month figure understates the run-rate; if it does not, the BUY signal weakens (see the thresholds above).
  • The unaccrued IRS exposure ($168.3M tax + $63.7M penalties, plus interest) and the $273.6M of purchase obligations are not deducted.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
