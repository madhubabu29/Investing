Model-based rating: NOT A BUY at this price — high risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Duolingo, the price requires about 5.0% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $253M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $84 per share at 10% (price $143.03).

What would change this: A price below about $124 would bring the required growth under 4% (HOLD) and below about $99 under 2% (BUY). At the current price, FCF after share-based pay above about $304M (now $253M) would move it to HOLD.

Main risk to the rating: Duolingo only became profitable recently: operating income was −$13.3M in 2023, and free cash flow after share-based pay was $55M that year. Share-based pay is about $145M a year, roughly 34% of operating cash flow, the highest in this group.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • No debt line was returned for Duolingo, so debt is treated as zero; if it has any, value per share would be lower.
  • 2023 capex excludes capitalized software, which was not retrieved for that year, so 2023 FCF is slightly overstated.
  • Customer deferred revenue is not subtracted; it is a liability that unwinds as service is delivered.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: 2025 plus first half 2026 minus first half 2025.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
