Business model

Duolingo is a freemium, gamification-first mobile and web learning platform, built around a free app that teaches languages (and, increasingly, math and music) in short, streak-driven lessons monetized through a small number of paid tiers layered on top of a very large free user base. Q2 2026 daily active users reached 58.7M (+23% YoY, re-accelerating two points sequentially), monthly active users reached 140.6M (+10% YoY), and paid subscribers reached 12.7M (+17% YoY) — still under 10% of MAUs, which is the core of the monetization bet: convert a small, growing slice of a huge free audience into Super Duolingo / Duolingo Max subscribers. Revenue splits roughly into subscriptions (the large majority, via the two subscription tiers), advertising shown to free users, the Duolingo English Test (a lower-cost, on-demand alternative to TOEFL/IELTS), and in-app purchases. The company has spent the past two years pushing beyond language: Duolingo Math is being built out as a parent-paid supplemental tutor positioned against apps like Kumon, and Duolingo Music (bolstered by the 2025 acqui-hire of game-design/audio studio NextBeat) is an earlier-stage bet on the same gamified-mastery format applied to a new subject. Layered on both is Duolingo Max, an AI-tutor tier that uses generative AI for features like conversational role-play and explain-my-answer, which management has framed as the centerpiece of a broader 2026 "Total Human Learning" product push.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a growth stock that fell 80% on an AI story it can't fully outrun

DUOL is a study in how fast a rich multiple can unwind. The stock peaked near $540 in May 2025 and traded around $147 as of Aug 28, 2026 — a roughly 73% decline from that high and a 57% decline over the trailing year, even as the underlying business kept growing revenue in the high-teens to 20%s and posted a Q2 2026 beat with DAU growth re-accelerating to 23%. Three overlapping stories are behind the collapse. First is reputational: in April 2025, CEO Luis von Ahn announced Duolingo would go "AI-first," replacing some contractors with AI-generated content and factoring AI adoption into hiring and performance reviews (on top of a prior 10% headcount cut); the backlash was severe enough that Duolingo wiped its TikTok and Instagram accounts in May 2025 after losing roughly 400,000 followers, and von Ahn walked the framing back within weeks, later dropping AI usage from performance reviews entirely. On the numbers, management itself said on the Q2 2025 call that DAU growth — while still strong — was "dampened" by the controversy, though the deceleration that followed (roughly 40% DAU growth in mid-2025 to 30% by Q4 2025, before re-accelerating to 23% in Q2 2026) is hard to cleanly separate from the natural math of compounding off an ever-larger base. Second and larger is a structural fear that isn't about Duolingo's execution at all: that general-purpose AI chatbots like ChatGPT are a free, always-available substitute for language practice, eroding the moat around a purpose-built app — a worry that resurfaces every time a competing AI product ships a new conversational feature. Third, management's own 2026 guidance — reaffirmed bookings growth of 10–12% and revenue growth of 15–18%, with gross margin guided down toward roughly 69–70% as AI inference costs rise — explicitly frames 2026 as an "investment year" prioritizing user growth over near-term monetization, which Wall Street has read as confirmation that the deceleration is real and not just optics; the average analyst price target as of mid-2026 (~$127) still implied further downside from even today's beaten-down price.

The debt picture

Duolingo carries no debt. As of June 30, 2026 it held $1,180.9M of cash and cash equivalents plus $102.7M of long-term investments — $1,283.6M combined, up from roughly $1,171.5M at 2025 year-end — against $0 of borrowings, for a net cash position of $1,283.6M ($27.44/share on 46.79M shares outstanding). Beta is a modest 0.88 (Robinhood and stockanalysis.com agree), lower than the stock's headline volatility over the past 18 months would suggest, likely reflecting that beta is measured against the market rather than against DUOL's own idiosyncratic AI-narrative swings. Applying CAPM (4.7% risk-free rate, 5% equity risk premium) with a debt-free capital structure puts the base-case WACC at roughly 9.1%.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Each scenario anchors on a different FCF base and growth path to bracket how much of Duolingo's recent deceleration is temporary versus structural. The base case uses the TTM run-rate and credits growth a step above management's own reaffirmed FY2026 guidance (10–12% bookings, 15–18% revenue) before decaying, on the view that the AI-first backlash and 2026 "investment year" framing are mostly noise around a still-compounding subscriber base. The conservative case starts from FY2025's actual FCF and assumes growth settles closer to the low end of guidance as the subscriber base matures and margin gets reinvested into AI compute costs. The bear case starts from FY2024's actual FCF — a full year before the AI-first controversy and before the tax-driven GAAP net income spike — and assumes growth decelerates faster and settles at a lower terminal rate, treating ChatGPT-style general-purpose AI as a genuine long-run competitive threat to the core language-practice use case rather than a one-time scare. Every scenario adds back net cash ($1,283.6M) and divides by 46.79M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

DUOL inverts the usual pattern in this series. Most names here have a market price sitting above every DCF scenario, pricing in growth a mechanical model can't reach; DUOL's $147.08 price sits between the bear case ($111.35) and the conservative case ($210.79) — after an 80%-from-peak decline, the market is now pricing something worse than a scenario built on FY2024's actual FCF decelerating to single-digit long-run growth. That is a specific, falsifiable bet: that the AI-first backlash, the 2026 investment-year margin give-up, and the ChatGPT-substitution risk will, in combination, push Duolingo's growth below even the bear case's 8%-then-3% path — not merely dent one or two quarters of DAU growth, which is what has actually shown up in the numbers so far (23% DAU growth in Q2 2026, an acceleration, not a further slide). If Duolingo's subscriber funnel and AI-tutor differentiation hold up anywhere near its own reaffirmed guidance, the conservative case alone implies over 40% upside from here; the bear case is the one that requires believing the reputational and competitive damage is more severe, and more durable, than anything management has reported to date.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC, and for a company this early in its margin-expansion curve it is also sensitive to how much of the recent FCF growth persists once AI-compute costs are fully reflected in gross margin. Stock-based compensation is a real distortion here: SBC ran $137.4M in FY2025 and $72.9M in H1 2026 alone — roughly a third of TTM free cash flow — so a meaningful share of the "cash" this DCF discounts is really compensation funded by shareholder dilution rather than economic profit available for buybacks or dividends; GAAP-FCF for a company this SBC-heavy overstates per-share value relative to what these numbers suggest at face value. FY2025's reported net income was also inflated by a one-time $256.7M non-cash tax benefit from releasing a deferred-tax valuation allowance, which is why trailing P/E looks unusually low; that item does not affect the FCF-based DCF here but would badly mislead an earnings-multiple comparison. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
