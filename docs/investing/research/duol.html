Model-based rating: NOT A BUY at this price — high risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Duolingo, the price requires about 5.3% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $239M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $82 per share at 10% (price $145.00).

What would change this: A price below about $120 would bring the required growth under 4% (HOLD) and below about $96 under 2% (BUY). At the current price, FCF after share-based pay above about $309M (now $239M) would move it to HOLD.

Main risk to the rating: Duolingo only became profitable recently: operating income was −$13.3M in 2023, and free cash flow after share-based pay was $55M that year. Share-based pay is about $157M over the latest twelve months, roughly 36% of operating cash flow, up from 34% in the previous version and the highest in this group. Twelve-month FCF after share-based pay slipped from $253M to $239M because share-based pay and capital spending both rose faster than operating cash flow.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • This update uses the third-quarter results letter filed with the SEC on Form 8-K (Nov 3, 2026), not the full Q3 10-Q, which I could not retrieve. The letter is unaudited and gives nine-month operating cash flow and share-based pay but not nine-month capital spending, so twelve-month capital spending is the prior twelve-month figure from the Q2 10-Q plus Q3 2026 less Q3 2025 from the letter.
  • 2023 capex excludes capitalized software, which was not retrieved for that year, so 2023 FCF is slightly overstated.
  • Customer deferred revenue is not subtracted; it is a liability that unwinds as service is delivered.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: 2025 plus nine months 2026 minus nine months 2025.
  • The price is the Nov 20, 2026 close of $145, as given by the site owner; I did not verify it against a market data source.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.

These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice; I am not a financial advisor.
