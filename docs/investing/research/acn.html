Model-based rating: HOLD — moderate risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Accenture, the price requires about 2.5% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $9.5B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $163 per share at 10% (price $221.17).

What would change this: A price below about $207 would bring the required growth to 2% or lower (BUY); a price above about $281 would push it over 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $340 over 5% (SELL). At the current price, FCF after share-based pay above about $10.2B a year (now $9.5B) would move it to BUY, and below about $7.5B to NOT A BUY.

Main risk to the rating: Cash returned to shareholders exceeded the free cash flow the business generated: buybacks of $7.5B and dividends of $4.0B in fiscal 2026 against FCF after share-based pay of $9.5B, funded by doubling long-term debt from $5.0B to $10.0B. Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Accenture's fiscal year ended Aug 31, 2026; the next report (Q1 fiscal 2027, quarter ending Nov 30) is not out yet, so the 10-K is the latest filing and no twelve-month derivation is needed.
  • The cover page states 668.7M Class A shares outstanding including 78.0M issued shares held by the company; I use the 590.7M that remain after deducting those. Using 668.7M instead would lower every per-share value by about 12%.
  • Net cash after noncontrolling interests is $1.0B. Counting debt only (not noncontrolling interests), net cash would be $2.7B.
  • Capital spending is purchases of property and equipment; no separate capitalized-software line was used.
  • Acquisition spending is not deducted from FCF; investing activities used $5.6B in fiscal 2026.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.

These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice; I am not a financial advisor.
