Business model

Pegasystems sells Pega Infinity, a low-code platform for building and running business process management (BPM), customer relationship management (CRM) and workflow-automation applications for large enterprises — banks, insurers, telcos, healthcare systems and government agencies that need to orchestrate complex, rules-heavy processes (loan origination, claims handling, customer service case management) across legacy systems. The commercial model runs on Annual Contract Value (ACV) — the annualized value of active subscription contracts — rather than one-time license sales, and Pega has spent the last several years pushing that ACV base from on-premise licenses toward Pega Cloud, its managed SaaS offering. Cloud ACV reached $926M in Q2 2026, up 22% year over year and now a majority of the roughly $1.6B total ACV book, with management's long-run target of Pega Cloud reaching 75%+ of total ACV. Layered on top since 2024 is Pega GenAI: Blueprint (an AI tool that turns a plain-language description of a workflow into a running application) and, more recently, Agentic Process Fabric, which orchestrates AI agents within Pega-built processes and is sold on a flat per-completed-case basis rather than per AI token consumed.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a litigation overhang that lifted, replaced by an AI-adoption air pocket

For nearly four years the single biggest question mark on Pega was Appian Corp. v. Pegasystems: a Virginia jury found in May 2022 that Pega misappropriated trade secrets and awarded Appian roughly $2.036B in damages plus a $2M injunction-related sanction — an amount that, if it had stood, would have dwarfed Pega's entire market cap. It did not stand. The Court of Appeals of Virginia reversed the verdict in July 2024 over multiple trial errors (chiefly a jury instruction that improperly shifted the burden of proving causation onto Pega) and ordered a new trial; the Supreme Court of Virginia unanimously affirmed that reversal in January 2026. Appian said the same month it intends to press the claims again at retrial, so the case is not closed, but as of this analysis there is no verdict, judgment or settlement on Pega's books — the $2B figure is not a real liability today, and the roughly $31.8M and $109.5M in narrower damages Appian is separately seeking in a related defamation suit (per Pega's Q1 2026 10-Q) are immaterial next to the original number. That overhang lifting has been overtaken, in practice, as the market's central concern: Pega's own Q1 and Q2 2026 results missed Wall Street estimates as "unprecedented changes in the AI market" — enterprise customers pausing new deals to figure out their own AI strategy and cost structure — slowed total ACV growth to 7-8% year over year in Q2, down from 12% the prior quarter, against a 15% full-year ACV guide management has not walked back. The stock is down roughly 47% from its October 2025 52-week high of $68.10 to the current $36.18, which is a re-rating around AI-driven demand-cycle uncertainty in the core business, not around litigation risk that has, for now, actually receded.

The debt picture

Pegasystems is effectively debt-free: its $600M convertible senior notes matured and were repaid in full (remaining principal and accrued interest of $469.6M) in Q1 2025, and no new debt has replaced them. As of the June 30, 2026 10-Q, the company held $185.1M of cash and equivalents plus $176.8M of current marketable securities — $361.9M combined, a net cash position of $2.20/share against 164.36M shares outstanding. That cushion shrank from $425.8M at year-end 2025 as Pega spent $367.7M repurchasing stock in H1 2026 (up from $251.7M in H1 2025) — a buyback pace that, at more than $600M annualized against 2025's $490.7M of FCF, is running ahead of current free cash flow generation and drawing down the cash balance to support the stock through the AI-demand air pocket. Beta is low at 0.86; using CAPM (4.7% risk-free rate, 5% equity risk premium) puts the cost of equity — and, with no debt, the WACC — at roughly 9.0%.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

The base case uses TTM FCF ($492.5M, through June 2026) and assumes ACV growth recovers toward management's 15% full-year guide before decaying — a materially more conservative single-year jump than the company's own $575M FY2026 FCF guidance implies. The conservative case starts from FY2025's actual FCF ($490.7M) and assumes the current AI-adoption pause persists longer, holding growth to mid-single digits. The bear case starts from the smoothed 5-year average FCF ($209.1M) — which still carries the weight of the 2021-2022 pre-cloud-transition years, including 2022's negative FCF — and assumes low-single-digit growth, treating the current ACV deceleration as the start of a durable slowdown rather than a one- or two-year pause, and implicitly pricing in some risk from the still-live Appian retrial. Every scenario adds net cash ($361.9M) and divides by 164.36M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

Every scenario except the bear case comes in well above today's price — even the conservative case, built on FY2025's actual FCF growing at a modest 6% decaying to 3.5%, implies $50.95/share, 41% above the $36.18 market price. That is an unusually wide gap for this series, and it says the market is pricing something closer to the bear case: not merely a pause in ACV growth, but either a genuine stall in the cloud transition, a real chance the Appian retrial produces a fresh damages award, or both. The historical FCF record actually supports real caution here — Pega's FCF was negative as recently as 2022 and the company has only four years of consistently positive free cash flow to point to, so a re-rating back toward that earlier, choppier profile if the AI-driven ACV slowdown proves structural rather than a one-year digestion pause is not a fringe scenario. Management's own aggressive buyback (spending more than TTM FCF on repurchases in H1 2026) reads as a bet that today's price undervalues the business; the DCF's spread suggests the market is instead betting that the last few years of margin expansion and ACV growth were the easy part, not the norm.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC. The Appian retrial is a binary risk not captured by smoothly declining growth rates in any scenario here — a fresh, sustained judgment against Pega, even far smaller than the original $2.036B verdict, would be a direct cash claim on a balance sheet with only $361.9M of net cash, and none of the three scenarios above reserve for that possibility. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
