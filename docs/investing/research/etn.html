Business model

Eaton is a global power management company, split into Electrical (Americas and Global) and Aerospace segments, plus a smaller Mobility group (Vehicle and eMobility) that is being separated out (see below). The Electrical business makes circuit breakers, switchgear, busway, transformers, and backup-power equipment used in data centers, utilities, and industrial buildings; Aerospace makes fuel, hydraulic, and motion-control systems for commercial and defense aircraft. Electrical and Aerospace combined generated about 88% of FY2025's $27.4B in sales at roughly 26% segment margins, and are the direct beneficiaries of the current AI-data-center buildout, grid modernization, and reshoring capex cycle — Eaton's backlog was up 33% year over year as of June 2026, with trailing-twelve-month orders up 41% organically.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a debt-funded M&A spree, a pending divestiture, and a rich multiple

Two large corporate actions are unfolding simultaneously in 2026. First, Eaton spent about $11B on acquisitions in Q1 2026 alone: $9.55B for Boyd Thermal (data-center thermal management, directly on-theme with the AI buildout) and $1.53B for Ultra PCS (aerospace electronics), funded by $8.5B of new senior notes issued in March 2026 (coupons 3.85%–5.45%, maturities 2028–2056) plus a $1B revolver upsize. Second, in June 2026 Eaton agreed to combine its Mobility group — Vehicle and eMobility, about 11% of FY2025 sales at a lower ~13% margin versus 26% for Electrical/Aerospace — with Dana Incorporated in a Reverse Morris Trust valued at over $10B, expected to close in Q1 2027; Eaton shareholders will hold at least 50.1% of the combined company and Eaton itself receives a roughly $1.1B cash distribution. The net effect: net debt roughly doubled, from $9.9B at FY2025-end to $20.6B by June 30, 2026, even as the company sheds its lowest-margin segment to become a more focused Electrical-and-Aerospace pure play. The market has rewarded the pivot — the stock is up sharply this year and trades near its 52-week high of $478, at a P/E near 46x, well above Eaton's historical 20–25x range — and management has raised full-year 2026 organic growth guidance twice, most recently to 11–13%.

The debt picture

Net debt stands at $20.6B ($53.12/share) as of June 30, 2026, up from $9.9B at the end of FY2025 — entirely a function of the Boyd Thermal and Ultra PCS acquisitions. GuruFocus puts Eaton's current WACC at 11.25%, 44% above its own 10-year median of 7.83%, reflecting both the added leverage and a beta that has drifted up to 1.18. Notably, GuruFocus's own framework flags that Eaton's 11.10% ROIC currently sits just below that 11.25% WACC — by their measure, the newest layer of growth is not yet clearing its cost of capital, even before crediting any synergies from the just-closed deals.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Each scenario anchors on a different FCF base: the TTM run-rate (which still absorbs Q1 2026's acquisition-integration cash drag), the smoothed 5-year average, and the FY2025 actual — the last full year before this year's M&A spree. The base case credits management's own newly-raised 2026 organic growth guidance (11–13%, using 11%) before decaying; the bear case treats the data-center capex supercycle the way MU and WDC's bear cases treat the memory/storage supercycle — as a boom that eventually cools. Every scenario subtracts net debt ($20.6B) and divides by 388.4M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

Even the base case — which already credits management's own newly-raised, top-of-cycle 11–13% growth guidance — lands 70% below today's price. This is the widest DCF-to-market gap of any name in this series, wider than either MU or WDC. The market isn't just pricing "strong growth continues"; every scenario here already assumes strong-to-very-strong growth. It's pricing a structurally higher multiple on top of that growth — a P/E near 46x versus Eaton's historical 20–25x range — on the bet that AI-data-center-driven demand for electrical equipment deserves a permanent re-rating, not just a cyclical earnings bump. Whether Eaton's pivot toward a more focused, higher-margin Electrical-and-Aerospace portfolio (once the Mobility separation closes) justifies that re-rating, or whether this is the same boom-extrapolation currently inflating MU and WDC, is a bet a mechanical DCF can't settle — it can only show that the multiple, not the cash flow, is doing almost all of the work in today's price.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC. This model values Eaton on today's consolidated cash flows, including the Mobility segment; the Dana Reverse Morris Trust hasn't closed yet (expected Q1 2027) and segment-level FCF isn't separately disclosed, so its removal isn't modeled directly — but at ~11% of sales and below-average margins, its departure would modestly improve blended margins, not close a 70%+ valuation gap. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
