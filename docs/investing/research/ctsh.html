Model-based rating: BUY (value signal) — moderate risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Cognizant, the price requires about 1.5% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $2,170M. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $49 per share at 10% (price $58.06).

What would change this: A price above about $62 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), above about $84 above 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $102 above 5% (SELL). The BUY is close to the line: the price is within about 7% of the HOLD threshold. At the current price, FCF after share-based pay falling below about $2.04B a year (now $2.17B) would also move it to HOLD, and below about $1.50B to NOT A BUY.

Main risk to the rating: Cash flow is not rising: nine-month operating cash flow was $1,836M against $2,025M a year earlier, and twelve-month FCF after share-based pay ($2.17B) is below the 2025 figure ($2.41B). Revenue growth in Q3 was 4.6%. The company is also spending on a restructuring program (Project Leap, $180M to $250M in total, $135M recorded so far), which may not be fully in the cash flow yet.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

  • The twelve-month figure is derived: 2025 plus nine months of 2026 minus nine months of 2025.
