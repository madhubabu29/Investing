What the data shows

Revenue growth has been modest — the 2021–2025 CAGR is 3.3%, with a rough patch in 2023 (-0.4%) and a rebound in 2025 (+7%). Free cash flow (FCF) has been volatile: it dipped to $1.83B in 2024, a weak year for IT services broadly, before recovering to $2.67B in 2025, driven by strong operating cash flow ($2.88B) and lean capex ($287M). The five-year average FCF margin is 11.2%.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

The scenarios differ on three levers: FCF growth rate, terminal growth rate, and WACC. The base case uses a published WACC of 7.9% and assumes growth stepping down from 6% to 4% as the company matures, with a 3% perpetual terminal rate. Every scenario adds net cash ($1.33B) and divides by 450.4M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

Even the bear case — flat-ish FCF growth and an 11% discount rate — implies $70/share vs. the market's $54.73. The market's 8.7× EV/FCF and 10.8% FCF yield only make sense if FCF declines roughly 3–5% per year indefinitely — an "AI disrupts IT outsourcing" scenario not captured in a standard DCF growth assumption. On pure financials, CTSH looks cheap; the market may be pricing in structural headwinds to the business model.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC — a 1% change in either can swing intrinsic value by 20–30%. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
