Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Garmin, the price requires about 7.1% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $1.3B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $88 per share at 10% (price $268.04).

What would change this: A price below about $162 would bring the required growth to 5% (NOT A BUY), below about $137 under 4% (HOLD), and below about $107 under 2% (BUY). At the current price, FCF after share-based pay above about $2.3B a year (now $1.3B) would move it from SELL to NOT A BUY, about $2.7B to HOLD, and about $3.7B to BUY.

Main risk to the rating: The price needs free cash flow to be about 1.8 times today's level just to justify 5% growth. Reported earnings are growing much faster than cash flow: operating income for the first 39 weeks of 2026 rose 31% ($1,659M vs $1,262M) and net sales 13%, but twelve-month FCF after share-based pay is only 8% above fiscal 2025, because purchases of property and equipment doubled to $295M in 39 weeks (from $146M); the filings used do not explain the increase. If earnings growth turns into cash flow, the picture changes. This rating compares the price with reported cash flow only; I did not assess business risks such as competition or tariffs.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • Marketable securities, including $1.73B held as non-current, are counted as cash-like.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2025 plus 39 weeks of 2026 minus 39 weeks of 2025.
  • Capital spending is purchases of property and equipment; no separate capitalized-software line was used.
  • The latest filing is the Q3 2026 10-Q. Garmin raised its full-year guidance with its Oct 28, 2026 results; the guidance itself is not part of the cash flow numbers used here.
  • Because Garmin has no debt and a large net cash balance, about $21 of the per-share value in each scenario is cash.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.
