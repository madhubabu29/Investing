Business model

InterDigital invents wireless and video patents (Wi-Fi, 5G, HEVC video compression, AI), then licenses them to device makers — Samsung, Apple, Huawei, Sony — and collects royalties while running on only 460 people. That produces $1.8M in revenue per employee and gross margins of 89%.

The defining challenge for this DCF — lumpiness

Revenue and FCF don't grow smoothly here; they lurch. When a major license deal comes up for renewal (or gets resolved through arbitration), a single agreement can move annual FCF by $150–200M. In 2024, revenue jumped 58% to $869M. In 2025, it dipped 4% to $834M but FCF nearly doubled to $529M as a large Samsung receivable converted to cash. Switching the base FCF assumption between 2025's peak ($529M) and the 5-year average ($283M) swings the intrinsic value by nearly 44%.

The debt picture

IDCC has $768M of net cash ($27.86/share). CapEx was just $16M in 2025 against $529M of FCF — a 3% reinvestment rate. The main "capex" for this business is R&D (building the next patent generation) and patent purchases ($63M in 2025), neither of which appears in the FCF calculation above.

What the scenarios say

The conservative case at $323 essentially matches today's price ($328) — the market is saying: we believe this FCF is real, but we want a 10% return and we don't trust rapid growth in a patent portfolio that depends on litigation outcomes and renewal timing. Use the 5-year average base ($283M) instead, and the conservative case drops to $186 — implying the stock is overvalued if 2025 was a cyclical peak.

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC, and here also to which base FCF year is chosen — a lumpy, deal-driven revenue base makes IDCC especially sensitive to that choice. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
