Method

Because the filings do not support a reliable multi-year operating forecast for a company this cyclical or lumpy, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0). Net cash is cash and short-term investments less the convertible notes.

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest filed quarter(s) annualized. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate.

Caveats

  • Patent purchases (about $63M in 2025 per the earlier version of this page) are not part of capex here, and research and development is already expensed in operating cash flow.
  • The convertible notes are treated as debt at their carrying amount; their conversion price and face value were not available in the data used.
  • H1 2026 share-based pay was not retrieved, so the run-rate uses half of the 2025 figure.
  • Operating cash flow depends on when licensees pay, so a single year can be far above or below revenue.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a company that keeps compounding will be worth more than shown.
  • Value is as of the latest filed balance sheet.

These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice; I am not a financial advisor.
