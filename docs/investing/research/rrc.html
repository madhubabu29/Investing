Model-based rating: NOT A BUY — high risk

Pending merger: On Oct 25, 2026, Range agreed to be acquired by EQT in an all-stock deal. Each Range share converts into 0.987 EQT shares, with no cash apart from fractional shares. Closing is expected in the first quarter of 2027, subject to shareholder votes at both companies and antitrust clearance (EQT 10-Q, Note 11). Range's price should therefore track EQT's price rather than Range's own cash flow. At $47.94, Range's price corresponds to about $48.57 per EQT share. This page values Range on its own cash flow only. It does not value EQT, and owning Range now means owning EQT after closing.

Rule used for cyclical companies: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value; SELL if it is more than twice the run-rate value (about $51). At 10%, Range's base value (3-year average FCF) is $12 and its run-rate value (twelve months to Sep 30, 2026) is $26. The $47.94 price is above both, and just under the SELL line. It needs about $1,200M of free cash flow every year, forever, against $678M over the last twelve months and $482M in 2025.

What would change this: a price below about $26 would be a HOLD, and below about $12 a BUY. A price above about $51 would be a SELL. At today's price, sustained FCF of about $1,200M a year would put the price at fair value on this method. Main risk to the rating: FCF swung from $262M in 2024 to $678M over the last twelve months. The 10-Q shows NYMEX gas averaging $3.57 per mcf in the first nine months of 2026 against $3.39 in 2025, and production up 6%. A fall in gas prices would cut the value sharply (see the sensitivity table).

What changed in this version

This page was rebuilt using only Range's SEC filings (the 2025 10-K and the Q3 2026 10-Q). Share-based compensation is treated as a real cost: FCF here is operating cash flow − capital spending − share-based and deferred compensation.

If you prefer companies with little or no debt, Range has a moderate amount: about 1.2 times twelve-month FCF after share-based pay.

Method

Because gas and liquids prices make Range's cash flow swing from year to year, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, minus net debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three years; Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, calculated as 2025 plus nine months of 2026 minus nine months of 2025. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate. Capex includes acreage purchases ($21.2M in nine months of 2026, $56.8M in 2025), which recur every year.

Caveats

  • Because of the merger, Range's share price is likely to follow EQT's. A standalone valuation of Range is only a partial guide to what happens to the price before closing, and the deal may not close (termination fees apply in certain cases).
  • The run-rate period includes nine months of gas prices a little above 2025. It is not a forecast.
  • The "deferred and stock-based compensation" add-back also includes the deferred compensation plan, so it may overstate pure share-based pay in some years. I deducted all of it.
  • Working capital used $138M of cash in the first nine months of 2026 (my sum of four lines), which lowers operating cash flow and therefore FCF.
  • Operating lease liabilities ($137.3M, about $0.59 a share) are not counted as debt. The extra $1.5B of 15-year transportation and processing commitments is not deducted either.
  • Any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a producer that grows volumes will be worth more than shown at the same prices.
  • The rating holds for any price between $26 and $51.
