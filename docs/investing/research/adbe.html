Business model

Adobe sells subscription software across Digital Media (Creative Cloud — Photoshop, Illustrator, Premiere — plus Document Cloud/Acrobat) and Digital Experience (enterprise marketing and analytics software), increasingly layered with generative AI tools under the Firefly brand. Revenue is almost entirely recurring subscription revenue, which has historically supported very high, sticky margins.

The defining question for this DCF — does the moat survive generative AI?

Unlike most stable compounders on this site, Adobe's normally-premium valuation has compressed hard — its P/E has fallen to roughly 14, unusually low for a company with 40%+ FCF margins and steady double-digit growth. The market is pricing real risk that generative AI tools, both AI-native startups and creative-agent features being built directly into general-purpose chatbots, could erode Adobe's must-have status and pricing power. Adobe's own response — the Firefly AI Assistant, a "creative agent" now embedded across Creative Cloud and also made available directly inside ChatGPT and Claude — is both a genuine growth lever (AI-related recurring revenue has already topped $500M) and an implicit hedge: making Firefly available inside rival AI products protects Adobe's position even if its own standalone apps become less central to the creative workflow. Competition varies by segment (Apple Final Cut and Blackmagic DaVinci in video, Canva and Figma in design, DocuSign in e-signature), and the company notes Core Digital Media growth has so far held up — meaning AI competition hasn't yet visibly hit retention or pricing.

The debt picture

Adobe's balance sheet is essentially balanced: $4.92B of cash against $4.80B of debt, a roughly breakeven $117M net cash position. Adobe has also been buying back stock aggressively — the share count has fallen from 418.6M a year ago to 397.5M now. WACC estimates vary (10-year median 9.03%, current GuruFocus read 10.97%), reflecting some recent volatility in the stock.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

The market price sits below even the bear case — a scenario that already assumes growth decays from 2% to flat and applies a 13% discount rate. In other words, the market isn't just pricing "growth slows down"; it's pricing something more pessimistic than that. This is a striking gap for a company whose FY2025 actuals (the shared base for all three scenarios) showed FCF growing 25% year over year and revenue growing a steady ~10.5% — there's no financial distress, no customer-concentration issue, and a clean balance sheet behind this valuation. The gap is a pure re-rating on competitive and AI-disruption fears, not a business-performance problem today. Whether that re-rating is a value opportunity or a correct pricing of tail risk comes down to a judgment call no DCF can make: does Adobe's moat hold up against generative AI creative tools the way it mostly held up against prior disruption waves (Canva, Figma), or is this one different in kind?

Caveat: DCF is extremely sensitive to the terminal growth rate and WACC — a 1% change in either can swing intrinsic value by 20–30%. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
