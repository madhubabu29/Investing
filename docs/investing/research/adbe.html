Model-based rating: BUY (value signal) — moderate risk

Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is above 4%. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Adobe, the price requires about 1.6% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $8.5B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $217 per share at 10% (price $263.83).

What would change this: A price above about $277 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), and above about $378 above 4% (NOT A BUY). At the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay falling below about $8.1B (now $8.5B) would also move it to HOLD.

Main risk to the rating: Share-based pay is about $2.1B a year, roughly 19% of operating cash flow, and fiscal 2025 buybacks ($11.3B) exceeded operating cash flow ($10.0B). This rating compares the price with reported cash flow only; I did not assess business risks such as competition.

Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • The data tool tagged long-term debt of $4.77B and current debt of $1.60B separately; I added them. If the $4.77B already included the current portion, debt would be about $1.3B lower and value per share about $3.5 higher.
