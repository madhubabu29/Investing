Rule used for this group: at a 10% discount rate, find the perpetual annual growth in free cash flow (after share-based pay) that the current price requires. BUY if that is 2% or lower, HOLD if it is between 2% and 4%, NOT A BUY if it is between 4% and 5%, and SELL if it is above 5%. Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. These thresholds are my own and not from any filing. For Adobe, the price requires about 0.0% growth forever on its latest twelve months of $8.5B. With no growth at all, the same cash flow is worth $217 per share at 10% (price $217.71).

What would change this: A price above about $277 would put the required growth above 2% (HOLD), above about $378 above 4% (NOT A BUY), and above about $458 above 5% (SELL). At the current price, twelve-month FCF after share-based pay falling below about $6.7B (now $8.5B) would move it to HOLD, and below about $4.9B to NOT A BUY.

Main risk to the rating: The price is almost exactly the no-growth value of the latest twelve months ($217 against $217.71), so the BUY rests on that $8.5B holding. Method

The value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed in the main table (g = 0), which is deliberately cautious for a business whose cash flow has been rising. The growth table below adds 2% and 4% perpetual growth on the latest twelve months, using value = FCF × (1 + g) ÷ (discount rate − g).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest twelve months.

Caveats

  • The latest filing is still the Q3 FY2026 10-Q (quarter ended Aug 28, 2026). Adobe's fiscal year ends Nov 27, 2026 and Q4 results are due in December, so they are not in these numbers. Buybacks after Aug 28 would lower both cash and the share count; at roughly fair prices that leaves value per share about unchanged.
  • An earlier version of this page flagged a possible double-count of the current portion of debt. The 10-Q shows it is a separate line from long-term debt, so the $6,363M total is correct.
  • Capital spending is purchases of property and equipment; no separate capitalized-software line was found.
  • The twelve-month figure is derived: fiscal 2025 plus nine months of fiscal 2026 minus the prior-year nine months.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; leases are not counted as debt.

These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice; I am not a financial advisor.
