Model-based rating: HOLD — moderate risk

Method for an insurer: I did not use free cash flow, because an insurer's operating cash flow mostly reflects premiums collected before claims are paid (the "float"), not profit available to shareholders. For example, 2025 operating cash flow was $10.6B against net income of $6.3B. Instead, the page values Travelers on net income after share-based pay, interest and taxes, discounted at 10% as a cost of equity. Debt is therefore not subtracted. Rule used for cyclical companies, applied to net income: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value; SELL if it is more than twice the run-rate value (about $813).

At 10%, Travelers' base value (3-year average net income of $4.8B) is $232 a share and its run-rate value (twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, $8.3B) is $407. The $393.24 price is between them, about 3% below the run-rate value. It needs about $8.1B of net income every year, forever, against $8.3B over the last twelve months. That is a price-to-earnings ratio of 9.7 on the latest twelve months and 2.8 times book value.

What would change this: a price above about $407 would be NOT A BUY, a price below about $232 a BUY, and a price above about $813 a SELL. Net income falling to about $8.1B would put the price at fair value. Main risk to the rating: the last twelve months were flattered by light catastrophe losses ($1.82B in nine months of 2026 against $3.60B in 2025) and by $1.23B of favorable prior-year reserve development. Net income was only $3.0B in 2023, and a normal or heavy storm year would cut the run-rate figure sharply (see the sensitivity table).

What changed in this version

This page was rebuilt using only Travelers' SEC filings (the 2025 10-K and the Q3 2026 10-Q). The earlier version used a CAPM-derived cost of capital (from a beta of 0.46) and an older price. Debt is $9.8B at Sep 30, 2026 (including a $750M issue of 4.95% notes due 2031 on Jul 24, 2026; a $200M 7.75% note was repaid on Apr 15), or 25% of debt plus shareholders' equity. Equity is lower than at year-end because of rising interest rates, not losses: shareholders' equity fell from $32.9B to $29.0B, with $4.6B of other comprehensive loss (unrealized losses on bonds) and $4.6B of buybacks outweighing $5.8B of net income. If you prefer companies with little or no debt, Travelers carries a moderate amount: about 1.2 times twelve-month net income, but it is a financial company, and debt-to-capital is the usual yardstick.

Method

Value per share = net income ÷ discount rate ÷ shares (flat perpetuity, g = 0). Net income is after interest, so debt is not subtracted. Four earnings levels are taken directly from the filings: Bear = the weakest of 2023–2025; Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the twelve months to Sep 30, 2026, calculated as 2025 plus nine months of 2026 minus nine months of 2025.

Caveats

  • Net income is not cash available to shareholders: insurers must hold capital against their risks, so only part of earnings can be paid out. In the first nine months of 2026, Travelers returned $5.3B (buybacks plus dividends), roughly its net income, while equity shrank because of the bond-price decline.
  • The run-rate period had light catastrophe losses and strong favorable reserve development. Both can reverse.
  • The twelve-month figure includes the fourth quarter of 2025, when the Canadian business that was sold on Jan 2, 2026 was still owned, so earnings going forward may be somewhat lower.
  • Net income includes realized investment gains and losses (about a $271M net loss after tax in nine months of 2026, taken as segment income less net income).
  • The unrealized bond losses ($8.1B gross) do not run through net income unless the bonds are sold or impaired, but they reduce shareholders' equity and so the capital available for buybacks.
  • Any growth beyond flat earnings is excluded, so a company that keeps growing premiums and investment income will be worth more than shown; future share buybacks are not modeled either (the share count used is the latest on the 10-Q cover).
