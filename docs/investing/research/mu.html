Model-based rating: HOLD — very high risk

Rule used for cyclical companies: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value; SELL if it is more than twice the run-rate value (about $2,417). Rating rule updated Nov 21, 2026: a SELL signal was added. At 10%, Micron's base value (3-year average) is $230 and its run-rate value (fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 annualized) is $1,209. The $595 price sits between them: it needs roughly $60.4B of free cash flow every year, forever, against $57.6B in fiscal 2026 and a fourth-quarter run-rate near $129.9B. In other words, the market is paying for the current memory boom to last, not for the through-cycle average.

What would change this: a price below about $230 would be a BUY on the 3-year average, and a price above about $1,209 would be NOT A BUY. Annualized FCF falling back toward $20B would argue for caution. Main risk to the rating: fiscal 2024 FCF was negative $0.7B after share-based pay, and fiscal 2025 was only $0.7B. FCF went from −$0.7B to +$57.6B in two years, so any value here is dominated by the cycle assumption.

Method

Because the filings do not support a reliable multi-year operating forecast for a company this cyclical or lumpy, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash and investments less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0).

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest filed quarter annualized. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate.

Caveats

  • The fourth-quarter run-rate is derived by subtracting the nine-month figures in the 10-Q from the full-year figures in the 10-K; it is a single quarter at record memory prices. Fiscal 2026 had 53 weeks; if the extra week fell in the fourth quarter, as is usual, a 13-week equivalent would be about 7% lower.
  • Long-term marketable investments ($30.0B) are counted as cash-like. If they were excluded, net cash would be $38.3B and every value above would be about $27 lower.
  • Capital spending was $30.7B in fiscal 2026, up from $15.9B, so FCF already reflects heavy investment, and about $4.2B more is committed.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a company that keeps compounding will be worth more than shown.
  • The rating holds for any price between $230 and $1,209.
