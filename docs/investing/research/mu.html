Model-based rating: HOLD — very high risk

Rule used: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value. At 10%, Micron's base value is $201 and its run-rate value is $1,180. The $877 price sits between them: it needs roughly $95.7B of free cash flow every year, forever, against $57.6B in fiscal 2026 and a fourth-quarter run-rate near $129.9B. In other words, the market is paying for the current memory boom to last, not for the through-cycle average.

What would change this: a price below about $200 would be a BUY on the 3-year average, while annualized FCF falling back toward $20B would argue for caution. Main risk to the rating: fiscal 2024 FCF was negative $0.7B after share-based pay, and fiscal 2025 was only $0.7B. FCF went from −$0.7B to +$57.6B in two years, so any value here is dominated by the cycle assumption.

Method

Because the filings do not support a reliable multi-year operating forecast for a company this cyclical or lumpy, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0). Net cash is cash and equivalents less the face value of the notes.

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest filed quarter(s) annualized. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate.

Caveats

  • The fourth-quarter run-rate is derived by subtracting the nine-month figures in the 10-Q from the full-year figures in the 10-K; it is a single quarter at record memory prices.
  • Capital spending was $30.7B in fiscal 2026, up from $15.9B, so FCF already reflects heavy investment.
  • Revenue was not available from the filing data tool, so no revenue history is shown.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a company that keeps compounding will be worth more than shown.
  • Value is as of the latest filed balance sheet.
