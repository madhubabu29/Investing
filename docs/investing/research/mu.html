Business model

Micron designs and manufactures memory and storage semiconductors — DRAM (including High Bandwidth Memory, HBM, used in AI accelerators) and NAND flash — sold to PC, mobile, datacenter, and automotive customers. It's one of only three major DRAM makers globally alongside Samsung and SK Hynix, which gives it real pricing power whenever the market tightens.

The defining challenge for this DCF — extreme cyclicality, mid-historic upswing

Memory is a brutally cyclical, capital-intensive commodity business, and the 5-year table above shows both extremes. FY2023 is what a downcycle looks like: revenue nearly halved and FCF went to −$6.1B as DRAM/NAND prices crashed on oversupply. FY2025–2026 is the opposite extreme: AI datacenter buildout has created a severe DRAM/HBM shortage — Micron's HBM capacity is completely sold out through 2026 — and fiscal Q3 2026 alone (Feb–May 2026) generated $41.46B of revenue, more than all of FY2025 combined, with gross margin surging from 39% to roughly 81% year over year. Trailing-twelve-month FCF (through Q3 FY2026) is approximately $26.2B — about 16 times FY2025's figure. Picking a "normal" FCF base here is almost a meaningless exercise; the company has swung from crisis to windfall in three years.

The debt picture

Micron has aggressively deleveraged during the upcycle. Total debt (including capital leases) fell from $14.6B at FY2025 year-end (Aug 28, 2025) to just $5.7B by Q3 FY2026 (May 28, 2026), while cash surged from $9.6B to $25.0B over the same nine months — flipping the balance sheet from $4.9B net debt to $19.3B net cash. WACC estimates vary enormously across sources (9.4% to 23.2%) — a reflection of just how volatile the stock has been amid this swing; the elevated end likely reflects current beta rather than a stable long-run cost of capital.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Given how extreme the range of plausible "normal" FCF is here, each scenario anchors on a very different base: the current TTM run-rate, a blend of TTM and the pre-supercycle FY2025 actual, and the 5-year average (which is dragged to near zero by the FY2023 crash). Every scenario adds net cash ($19.27B) and divides by 1,129.4M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

This is the mirror image of most other pages on this site: here, even the bullish base case falls dramatically short of the market price. Using the extraordinary current TTM FCF as a perpetual base with continued (moderating) growth only supports $299/share — 66% below today's $877.57. Pushed further: annualizing the single record Q3 FY2026 quarter ($17.6B of quarterly FCF × 4) and holding it flat forever at a 10.5% discount rate only reaches about $666/share, still 24% below market. The market isn't just pricing in the current supercycle persisting — it's pricing in further growth from here (continued HBM/DRAM price increases, capacity additions, the HBM4 ramp), a bet that historically boom-bust memory markets have never sustained indefinitely.

Caveat: DCF is a poor natural fit for a company in the middle of what may be a once-in-a-cycle structural shift (AI-driven memory demand) layered on top of an already famously cyclical industry. The gap between the crash-level bear case and the supercycle base case is enormous by design — these scenarios bracket genuine uncertainty about whether this cycle is structurally different, not a forecast of which one is right.
