Model-based rating: NOT A BUY at this price — very high risk

Rule used: BUY if the price is below the base-case value at 10%; HOLD if it is between the base and run-rate values; NOT A BUY if it is above the run-rate value. At 10%, Western Digital's base value is $30 and its run-rate value (latest quarter annualized) is $140. The $344 price is above both. It needs about $12.8B of FCF a year after share-based pay forever, against $5.2B at the latest quarter's pace and $3.3B in fiscal 2026.

What would change this: quarterly FCF sustained above about $3B (about $12B a year), or a price below roughly $140. Main risk to the rating: storage demand and pricing have been the strongest in years, so an investor who expects the boom to continue and grow would value it higher than this flat model does.

Method

Because the filings do not support a reliable multi-year operating forecast for a company this cyclical or lumpy, the value is the present value of a flat perpetual stream of FCF (after share-based pay) at four levels taken directly from the filings, plus cash less debt, divided by shares. No growth is assumed (g = 0). Net cash is cash and equivalents less the carrying amount of debt.

The four levels are: Bear = the weakest of the last three fiscal years (floored at zero); Conservative = the median year; Base = the 3-year average; Run-rate = the latest filed quarter(s) annualized. Present value = FCF ÷ discount rate.

Caveats

  • Net income of $9.4B is far above operating income of $4.5B, which suggests large non-operating gains, possibly tied to the 2025 Sandisk spin-off. I could not verify the size of any retained investment from the data available, so none is counted here; if WDC holds sellable stakes, value would be higher.
  • Fiscal 2024 and 2025 figures are as restated for continuing operations (the HDD business) in the FY2026 10-K.
  • The 3.00% convertible notes were largely converted in the quarter; the remaining $141M is treated as debt.
  • Cash and debt are taken at the dates shown; any growth beyond flat FCF is excluded, so a company that keeps compounding will be worth more than shown.
  • Value is as of the latest filed balance sheet.
