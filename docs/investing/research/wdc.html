Historical financials (actuals, continuing operations only)

Western Digital completed the spinoff of its NAND flash business (SanDisk Corp) in 2025 and restated prior years in its FY2025 10-K to show continuing (HDD-only) operations. Only 3 comparable years exist on this basis — the pre-spinoff combined-company figures included the divested Flash business and aren't comparable, so they're excluded here.

Business model

Western Digital makes hard disk drives (HDDs) — primarily nearline/enterprise drives sold to cloud and hyperscale datacenter customers, plus some consumer and client storage. Enterprise HDDs remain roughly seven times cheaper than SSDs for bulk AI-scale storage, which has made WDC a concentrated, pure-play way to invest in what analysts are calling a storage supercycle following the SanDisk spinoff.

The defining challenge for this DCF — a spinoff and a supercycle at once

Within the short 3-year comparable window, WDC went from FCF-negative in FY2023–2024 (spinoff transition costs plus a soft HDD market) to a sharp positive inflection in FY2025 ($1.28B) as enterprise AI datacenter demand for storage capacity surged. That trend has only accelerated since: WDC's entire 2026 HDD production capacity is sold out, with supply contracts locked in through 2028, and quarterly revenue has climbed every quarter this fiscal year (Q1 FY2026 $2.82B → Q2 $3.02B → Q3 $3.34B). Trailing-twelve-month FCF (through April 2026) is $2.9B — more than double FY2025's already-strong figure, with FCF margin expanding from 13.4% to roughly 25% over that stretch.

The debt picture

The balance sheet has transformed just as fast as the income statement. WDC had $2.6B of net debt at FY2025 year-end (June 27, 2025); by Q3 FY2026 (April 3, 2026) that flipped to a small $469M net cash position, as total debt was paid down from $4.7B to $1.6B in nine months, funded directly by the FCF inflection above. WACC estimates range widely (8.1% to 19.3% across sources), again reflecting how volatile the stock has been through this swing.

DCF inputs and results — three scenarios

Each scenario anchors on a different FCF base to bracket the uncertainty: the current TTM run-rate, the FY2025 actual, and a thin-but-positive "structural" estimate for the bear case (the raw 3-year average is deeply negative, but that's skewed by one-time spinoff separation costs in FY2023–2024, not representative of steady-state HDD economics). Every scenario adds net cash ($469M) and divides by 344.68M shares.

The uncomfortable question the DCF raises

As with Micron, this is the inverse of the usual finding on this site: even the base case — TTM FCF growing 8% decaying to 4%, at a 9.2% discount rate — implies only $172/share, 60% below the $434.30 market price. The market is pricing in a continuation and further acceleration of the storage supercycle well beyond what even the current elevated run-rate supports under a standard DCF. "Sold out through 2028" bookings make that bet more credible than usual, but HDDs — like DRAM — have a long history of boom-bust cycles once new supply eventually catches up with demand.

Caveat: The FY2023–2025 history is short and structurally messy (spinoff transition costs, a demand inflection, and a business-model change all inside 3 years), so "normal" FCF is unusually hard to pin down here. These are mechanical estimates from reported data, not investment advice.
